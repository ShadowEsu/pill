The box measures.
Pill explains.

A tablet cannot lie about dissolving.A tablet cannot lie about dissolving.

A dissolution check the size of a coffee mug, from hobby parts: a 3D-printed chamber, three LEDs, two light sensors and an Arduino UNO Q. Drop one tablet in a clear cup of water, close the lid, and 60 s later (quick run; 2–10 min for a full one) it says Passed the screen, Needs lab testing or Unable to verify. In a clinic, that answer decides whether the supplier gets paid.

02Why it has to exist.

The problem

One in ten.

WHO estimates that 1 in 10 medical products in low- and middle-income countries is substandard or falsified. A model cited by WHO puts the cost at about 116,000 extra malaria deaths a year in sub-Saharan Africa from substandard and falsified antimalarials alone. The instrument that catches this in a lab costs $20,000 to $100,000 and sits far from the clinics that need it.

Why a lookup is not enough

Lot numbers can be copied. Dissolution cannot.

In December 2025 the FDA found falsified Ozempic inside the legitimate US supply chain, labelled with a lot number that was also a real one. A recall lookup alone passes that product. A measurement of the tablet itself is the only check that does not trust the label. FDA, Dec 2025

White, blue and red 5 mm LEDs push into holes in the box's outer wall and shine through a diffuser plate that slides into a slot on top. Lit in turn once a second; red is the colour reference.

04What one run looks like.

Fig 2 · One run

One tablet, start to answer.

before → 60 s (quick) · 2–10 min (full)

Before

Good tablets make the reference.

Three or more runs of a known-good tablet on this same station make a reference profile: how fast it dissolves and how far the signal moves, colour (blue minus red) for yellow tablets, cloudiness on the side sensor for white ones. Without a profile the answer is Unable to verify, by design. The station’s first profile is not recorded yet.

Bottle

Photograph the bottle.

Drug, strength, lot and maker come off the label. The lot is checked against openFDA enforcement reports; the answer is shown as no record for this lot, recall, or lookup unavailable. Never "clear".

Clean water · 10 s

Dark, then blank.

Every second, every LED goes off for a dark reading that cancels room light. Clean water in the cup has to read steady for 10 s. If the lid is open or the blank is noisy, the run does not start.

Tablet in

Drop one tablet. Stir by hand.

Opening the lid marks the moment the tablet went in; open longer than 25 s and the run says Unable to verify. The inline sensor watches how much light gets through; the 90° sensor watches how much scatters sideways as the water clouds.

60 s · or 2–10 min

The curve is matched. Pill answers.

Quick mode: the first 60 s are compared with the reference curve at 5 s checkpoints. Full mode runs until the curve flattens, then checks time-to-80 % and the final level against the spread of good runs. One of three answers, with the reason.

    06What the judges see.

    Fig 2c · Pill Gate, the station app

    At the table.

    The station on the table runs Pill Gate: a guided check that walks one tablet from clean water to an answer in five phases, with the time each one takes on the rail.

    1. PreparechecksClean water in the cup, lid closed, LED reaching the sensor.
    2. Clean water10 sThe reading has to stay steady for 10 s before a tablet goes in.
    3. Tablet in≈ 10 sOpen the lid, drop the tablet, close it. The lid opening is the tablet-in mark; open past 25 s, the run says Unable to verify.
    4. Dissolving60 sQuick mode: 60 s after the tablet goes in. Full mode stops when the curve flattens, 2–10 min.
    5. ResultautoMatched against the reference profile; one of three answers.

    Screens from Pill Gate, replaying a recorded run (the top bar says REPLAY (not live))

    The station

    An Arduino UNO Q runs at 3.3 V with a 12-bit ADC. Once a second it turns every LED off and reads both photoresistors (the dark reading, which cancels room light), then lights blue, white and red in turn and reads again: one photoresistor in line with the beam, one at 90° to it. It prints one CSV line and decides nothing. Pill Gate, on the laptop, builds the curves and matches the run against a reference profile recorded from known-good tablets on the same station.

    Three answers, one product

    • Passed the screenPASS
    • Needs lab testingNEEDS LAB
    • Unable to verifyUNABLE TO VERIFY

    The station says it in words; the clinic app writes the same three answers as PASS / NEEDS LAB / UNABLE TO VERIFY.

    07What it says back.

    Fig 3 · The clinic app

    Three answers.
    Never a guess.

    The clinic app runs on a laptop and on your phone. Green means the run matched the reference. Warm red means it did not, and the money stays put. Grey means Pill has nothing to compare against, so it asks a person. The phone below replays a simulated curve.

    • P1PASS (Passed the screen): the run sits inside the spread of good tablets on this station.
    • N1NEEDS LAB (Needs lab testing): outside it, slower, weaker, cloudier or clearer than the good ones.
    • U1UNABLE TO VERIFY: no reference for this product, a fault, the lid open too long, or a noisy blank.

    08Where the answer holds the money.

    Clinic mode

    The verdict holds the payment.

    A clinic samples one tablet from each delivery. PASS proposes release; NEEDS LAB holds the money and drafts a lab referral; UNABLE TO VERIFY escalates. Every decision is logged with the lot, the time, the rule that fired and the policy clause it quotes. No money moves without a pharmacist's tap. Today every money move is simulated: USDC on Base Sepolia once a CDP key is in, with a Capital One Nessie sandbox mirror if its key lands.

    policy.md · the six clauses the agent may quote

    • P1Release needs PASS, no recall record for the lot, amount within the spend cap, and the pharmacist's tap.
    • P2NEEDS LAB: hold, and draft the lab referral with the curve attached.
    • P3UNABLE TO VERIFY: escalate to a person; no money moves.
    • P4A recall hit on the lot: hold, whatever the curve says.
    • P5Amount above the cap: escalate.
    • P6Recall lookup unreachable: hold until a lookup succeeds or a person overrides.

    09How the money moves.
    Coinbase · x402 · payment agents

    Payments

    The verdict is the invoice.

    Money in Pill is boring on purpose. The clinic pays a delivery into escrow when it is logged. The station tests one tablet. The policy agent reads the verdict, the recall check and the spend cap, and proposes release, hold or escalate. A pharmacist taps. Only then does USDC move from escrow to the supplier, on Base, signed by the agent's own CDP wallet. The same agent can buy a verdict from someone else's station with x402, a few cents at a time.

    Fig 6 · The tree

    How the money moves, and who decides.

    Measure, three answers, the policy agent, then Coinbase. Under it, how an AI agent pays for a verdict over Coinbase x402. Ink arrows are built; the dashed one is not yet run.

    One rail, simulated todayUSDC on Base Sepolia through a CDP agent wallet; nothing moves until the CDP key is in. A Capital One Nessie sandbox mirror is built for the fiat story and runs only if its key lands. One log line per decision.
    What the agent can doPropose, draft a referral, buy a verdict under $1. It cannot release: release is a human tap, every time, and the model can only downgrade a decision.
    What the agent cannot doHold more than one delivery's value, pay anyone not on the delivery, or move anything on a NEEDS LAB or UNABLE TO VERIFY. The caps live in policy.md, in plain text.

    10At home, one bottle at a time.

    Personal mode

    One tablet per bottle.

    Photograph a new bottle, test one tablet from it, and Pill keeps the result in My medicines with the recall check and a one-tap dose log. It never nags, never tells you to stop a medicine, and says plainly that measuring a dose needs the station.

    what it does

    • 01Reads the label: drug, strength, lot, maker, expiry. Fields stay editable; the photo is deleted after extraction.
    • 02Checks the lot against openFDA recalls, live or from a dated cache, and says which.
    • 03Keeps one verdict per bottle, from the one tablet you tested, with its two numbers and rule id.
    • 04Logs a dose with one tap. Recall alerts arrive later if the lot is added to the list.

    what it never does

    • —Tell you to stop a medicine, or tell you a tablet is fine to take.
    • —Re-test from the list. Measuring a dose destroys a tablet and needs the station.
    • —Send your photos anywhere. Only the five label fields leave the phone.
    11What it will not claim.

    Fig 5 · What PASS does not mean

    Pill screens tablets. A lab has the last word.

    PASS means

    This one tablet, on this station, dissolved like the good tablets in its reference profile. No red flags found.

    PASS does not mean

    That the tablet is the right drug, the right dose, or free of contaminants. Pill checks how a tablet dissolves; it does not identify ingredients. Anything it cannot compare goes to a lab, by design.

    Six questions we expect at the table

    Ask us the hard ones.

    "Why aspirin?"

    It is white, so it has no colour to measure, which is why the side sensor matters: on our first run its cloudiness signal rose by 0.29 against a blank noise of 0.006, while the red channel moved by 0.035. Riboflavin was the first plan, but it absorbs blue so strongly that a whole tablet saturates the cup.

    "Would this catch a real antimalarial?"

    Not today, and it says so: UNABLE TO VERIFY for any drug without a reference profile on this station. An honest "no reference" beats a guess.

    "How do we know the demo isn't rigged?"

    Run ids and timestamps on every curve, the raw trace saved for every run, and a station you can open and look inside. Bring your own tablet.

    "Who lets software hold money?"

    Nobody. The policy proposes; a pharmacist taps. The model can only downgrade a decision to "escalate", never release.

    "Why not just scan the barcode?"

    Because lot numbers get copied. The FDA's December 2025 Ozempic case carried a real lot number. Only the tablet itself cannot lie about dissolving.

    "What does it cost, really?"

    Hobby parts: an Arduino UNO Q, two photoresistors, three LEDs, a clear cup and a 3D-printed chamber. One tablet per test and 60 s for a quick run. A new lab dissolution tester typically costs $20,000–$100,000, and a lab result typically takes 7–10 business days.

    The team

    Built in one weekend, by hand.

    Every line of firmware, backend and app, the station, the first runs and this page were made during the Cal Hacks 13.0 hacking period. The method is textbook optics: a tablet changes how light crosses a cup of water; two sensors watch it happen. What is new is what the station does with the answer.

    where AI is used, and where it is not

    • AIClaude reads the bottle label into five fields, writes the two-sentence "why" under a verdict, and drafts the decision-log line, all passed through a word filter.
    • NOTThe verdict, the seven rules, the pass band, and every money movement are deterministic code. The model can only downgrade to "escalate".
