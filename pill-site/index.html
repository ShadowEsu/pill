02Why it has to exist.

The problem

One in ten.

WHO estimates that 1 in 10 medical products in low- and middle-income countries is substandard or falsified. A model cited by WHO puts the cost at about 116,000 extra malaria deaths a year in sub-Saharan Africa from substandard and falsified antimalarials alone. The instrument that catches this in a lab costs $20,000 to $100,000 and sits far from the clinics that need it.

Why a lookup is not enough

Lot numbers can be copied. Dissolution cannot.

In December 2025 the FDA found falsified Ozempic inside the legitimate US supply chain, labelled with a lot number that was also a real one. A recall lookup alone passes that product. A measurement of the tablet itself is the only check that does not trust the label. FDA, Dec 2025

04What one run looks like.

Fig 2 · One run

Three minutes, start to verdict.

night before → 3:00

Night before

Ten reference runs make the band.

Same cup, same fill line, the same stir, water at about 25 °C. Ten riboflavin quarter-tablets, three chalk tablets, two unknowns. The pass band is the mean ± 3 SD of the ten plateaus, checked leave-one-out. If the runs disagree by more than 15%, there is no demo, and we say so.

0:00

Photograph the bottle.

Drug, strength, lot and maker come off the label. The lot is checked against the FDA recall list; the answer is shown as no record for this lot, recall, or lookup unavailable. Never "clear".

0:20

Dark, then blank.

Lights off for a dark reading. Plain water for a blank. If the lid is open or the blank is unstable, the run does not start.

0:40

Drop one tablet. Stir.

The inline sensor watches blue light fade as riboflavin dissolves. The 90° sensor watches the light that comes out sideways. The water temperature goes in with the run.

3:00

The curve flattens. Pill decides.

The plateau is compared with last night's ten reference runs on this same box. Seven rules, checked in order. The rule that fired is printed on the card.

    06What the judges see.

    Fig 2c · Pill Gate, the station app

    At the table.

    The box on the table runs Pill Gate: a guided check that walks one tablet from clean water to an answer in five phases, with the time each one takes on the rail.

    1. PreparechecksClean water in the cup, lid closed, LED reaching the sensor.
    2. Clean water10 sThe reading has to stay steady for 10 s before a tablet goes in.
    3. Tablet in≈ 10 sOpen the lid, drop the tablet, close it. The lid opening is the tablet-in mark; open past 25 s, the run says Unable to verify.
    4. Dissolving60 sQuick mode: 60 s after the tablet goes in. Full mode stops when the curve flattens, 2–10 min.
    5. ResultautoMatched against the reference profile; one of three answers.

    Screens from Pill Gate, replaying a recorded run (the top bar says REPLAY (not live))

    The station

    An Arduino UNO Q runs at 3.3 V with a 12-bit ADC. Once a second it turns every LED off and reads both photoresistors (the dark reading, which cancels room light), then lights blue, white and red in turn and reads again: one photoresistor in line with the beam, one at 90° to it. It prints one CSV line and decides nothing. Pill Gate, on the laptop, builds the curves and matches the run against a reference profile recorded from known-good tablets on the same box.

    Three answers, one product

    • Passed the screenPASS
    • Needs lab testingNEEDS LAB
    • Unable to verifyUNABLE TO VERIFY

    The station says it in words; the clinic app writes the same three answers as PASS / NEEDS LAB / UNABLE TO VERIFY.

    08Where the answer holds the money.

    Clinic mode

    The verdict holds the payment.

    A clinic samples one tablet from each delivery. PASS proposes release; NEEDS LAB holds the money and drafts a lab referral; UNABLE TO VERIFY escalates. Every decision is logged with the lot, the time, the rule that fired and the policy clause it quotes. No money moves without a pharmacist's tap. Payments run on the Capital One Nessie sandbox: simulated money, real workflow.

    policy.md · the six clauses the agent may quote

    • P1Release needs PASS, no recall record for the lot, amount within the spend cap, and the pharmacist's tap.
    • P2NEEDS LAB: hold, and draft the lab referral with the curve attached.
    • P3UNABLE TO VERIFY: escalate to a person; no money moves.
    • P4A recall hit on the lot: hold, whatever the curve says.
    • P5Amount above the cap: escalate.
    • P6Recall lookup unreachable: hold until a lookup succeeds or a person overrides.

    09How the money moves.
    Coinbase · x402 · payment agents

    Payments

    The verdict is the invoice.

    Money in Pill is boring on purpose. The clinic pays a delivery into escrow when it is logged. The box tests one tablet. The policy agent reads the verdict, the recall check and the spend cap, and proposes release, hold or escalate. A pharmacist taps. Only then does USDC move from escrow to the supplier, on Base, signed by the agent's own CDP wallet. The same agent can buy a verdict from someone else's box with x402, a few cents at a time.

    Two rails, one ledgerUSDC on Base Sepolia through a CDP agent wallet, mirrored in the Capital One Nessie sandbox for the fiat story. One log line per decision, same fields on both.
    What the agent can doPropose, draft a referral, buy a verdict under $1. It cannot release: release is a human tap, every time, and the model can only downgrade a decision.
    What the agent cannot doHold more than one delivery's value, pay anyone not on the delivery, or move anything on a NEEDS LAB or UNABLE TO VERIFY. The caps live in policy.md, in plain text.

    10At home, one bottle at a time.

    Personal mode

    One tablet per bottle.

    Photograph a new bottle, test one tablet from it, and Pill keeps the result in My medicines with the recall check and a one-tap dose log. It never nags, never tells you to stop a medicine, and says plainly that measuring a dose needs the station.

    what it does

    • 01Reads the label: drug, strength, lot, maker, expiry. Fields stay editable; the photo is deleted after extraction.
    • 02Checks the lot against openFDA recalls, live or from a dated cache, and says which.
    • 03Keeps one verdict per bottle, from the one tablet you tested, with its two numbers and rule id.
    • 04Logs a dose with one tap. Recall alerts arrive later if the lot is added to the list.

    what it never does

    • —Tell you to stop a medicine, or tell you a tablet is fine to take.
    • —Re-test from the list. Measuring a dose destroys a tablet and needs the station.
    • —Send your photos anywhere. Only the five label fields leave the phone.
    11What it will not claim.

    Fig 5 · What PASS does not mean

    Pill screens tablets. It does not certify them.

    PASS means

    The dissolution curve of this one tablet, on this box, was consistent with last night's riboflavin reference runs. No red flags found.

    PASS does not mean

    That the tablet is the right drug, the right dose, or free of contaminants. Pill has one reference drug today. Anything it cannot compare goes to a lab, by design.

    Six questions we expect at the table

    Ask us the hard ones.

    "Why riboflavin?"

    It is the one tablet we could calibrate overnight: it absorbs blue light and glows green, so two sensors can disagree about chalk. The method is the same for any drug once its reference runs exist.

    "Would this catch a real antimalarial?"

    Not today, and it says so: UNABLE TO VERIFY for any drug without a reference on this box. One reference drug, honestly labelled, beats a guess.

    "How do we know the demo isn't rigged?"

    Run ids and timestamps on every curve, the raw trace saved for every run, and an ugly box you can open. Bring your own tablet.

    "Who lets software hold money?"

    Nobody. The policy proposes; a pharmacist taps. The model can only downgrade a decision to "escalate", never release.

    "Why not just scan the barcode?"

    Because lot numbers get copied. The FDA's December 2025 Ozempic case carried a real lot number. Only the tablet itself cannot lie about dissolving.

    "What does it cost, really?"

    $40 in parts, a quarter tablet per test, three minutes. A lab tester starts at $20,000 and a result takes 7–10 business days.

    The team

    Built in one weekend, by hand.

    Every line of firmware, backend and app, the station, the calibration runs and this page were made during the Cal Hacks 13.0 hacking period. The method is textbook optics: a known tablet absorbs a known colour; two sensors watch it happen. What is new is what the box does with the answer.
