## Patterns (10 lines)
1. The top overall prizes go to **physical hardware**: Peel (HackMIT '26 #1), CareChair (#3), PSP (HackHarvard '25 #1), ResQme (#2). Each used a cheap sensor or microcontroller next to a laptop.
2. Typical input: a **sensor or camera, plus voice for the user** (Deepgram/ElevenLabs). Text-only apps mostly win sponsor prizes, not overall.
3. **Health wins most often.** Peel, CareChair, PSP, Halo and the many Regeneron prizes all have a clear victim and a clear moment of use.
4. **Disaster / early warning wins:** ResQme (ESP32 mesh + GPS SOS), Trace (warns 8.7 h ahead), Eyrie (crowd-crush warning), Kava (wildfire claims). That matches "before it comes" exactly.
5. ESP32 shows up in Peel, Klick and ResQme. Your ESP32 + GPS + DHT11 + buzzer kit is enough for a ResQme/Trace-style project.
6. **Sponsor stacking works.** Peel entered 10 challenges and wrote one paragraph per sponsor; CareChair won 3 sponsor prizes plus 3rd overall; Klick won 2.
7. Fintech wins come from **sponsor tracks** (Visa, Capital One, Ramp, Maximor), not overall placings. Fraud and MFA dominate.
8. Winning demos show a **number as proof**: "$30 vs five figures", "warned every destroyed home, 8.7 h ahead", "<$100 of parts".
9. Teams show **honesty guardrails**: "unable to verify" states, cited evidence, double opt-in. Judges reward trustworthy AI.
10. The common shape: device reads the world → app/website turns readings into a decision → a voice/SMS alert reaches a person. That is the pipeline you want.
