# How Preston sounds (voice note for the Pill Devpost)

1. He talks in quick bursts, often dictated: one long run-on, then a two-word line ("6 now", "are we winning?", "switch claude 5").
2. Plain words, no corporate tone. "stuff", "overall", "pretty", "way too basic", "hella", "and stuff like that".
3. He repeats words for push, not for style: "keep testing, testing, testing", "really, really, really good", "improve, improve, improve".
4. He always wants more and better: "upgrade the app in every way", "more animations, more everything", "keep upgrading".
5. He is honest about what is not good yet: "it looks pretty plain", "I don't think this is good enough", "Actually real, please."
6. He cares that it is real: hardware connected to a backend, "not just our app with no connection".
7. He is warm and a little goofy: "it was brilliant, gentlemen", domain jokes like "Don't swallow that pill".
8. Excited and competitive, but it comes out as energy, not hype words: "make sure we are first place", "go for a lot of tracks, one by one".
9. Polite even when rushed: "please" in almost every message.
10. He thinks out loud with hedges a real person uses: "maybe Figma, maybe security", "I don't think we can do fintech with this, but".
11. Tonight, about the weekend: "I had so much fun in the hackathon, I'm so excited about everything, and I really miss this experience."
12. His physics is a school-lab memory (nickel(II) by colour, clear water as the blank), told plainly, not as a lecture.
13. For Devpost: fix his typos, keep his rhythm. Short sentences next to long ones. First person plural "we", sometimes "I".
14. Let the fun show two or three times (the UNO Q surprise, the riboflavin flop, the first aspirin run). Say "fun" at most once.
15. End sections on a concrete fact or a plain ask ("try to make it say PASS on the chalk"), never a send-off.

# Tell-list checklist (humanizer skill, patterns 1-33 + Devpost-specific)

- [ ] No significance puffing: "stands as", "pivotal", "testament", "evolving landscape", "marks a shift" (§1)
- [ ] No -ing tails: "highlighting", "showcasing", "ensuring", "reflecting" (§3)
- [ ] No promo words: "groundbreaking", "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "stunning", "vibrant" (§4)
- [ ] No vague authorities: "experts say", "studies show" without the named source (§5)
- [ ] No "Despite these challenges" outline section (§6)
- [ ] No AI vocabulary: delve, leverage, crucial, key (adj), enhance, foster, showcase, underscore, tapestry, landscape, additionally (§7)
- [ ] Use is/are/has, not "serves as", "boasts", "features" (§8)
- [ ] No "not just X, but Y" / "it's not X, it's Y" / trailing "no guessing" (§9)
- [ ] Break forced triplets; use two, or four, or one (§10)
- [ ] No synonym cycling for the same thing (station / device / box / unit) (§11)
- [ ] No fake "from X to Y" ranges (§12)
- [ ] No subjectless fragments ("No setup needed.") (§13)
- [ ] Zero em dashes and en dashes (— –); use periods, commas, colons, parentheses; ranges as "2 to 10 min" (§14)
- [ ] Bold only where the form needs it; no bold-header bullet lists (§15, §16)
- [ ] Sentence-case headings unless the Devpost field name is fixed (§17)
- [ ] No emojis, straight quotes only (§18, §19)
- [ ] No chatbot artifacts, no sycophancy (§20, §22)
- [ ] No filler ("in order to", "it is important to note") or stacked hedges (§23, §24)
- [ ] No generic upbeat closer ("the future looks bright") (§25)
- [ ] No "at its core", "the real question", "let's dive in" (§27, §28)
- [ ] No one-line restatement under a heading (§29)
- [ ] No staccato fragment runs or aphorisms ("Honesty is the product.") (§31, §32)
- [ ] No "Honestly?" / "Here's the thing" openers; no rhetorical questions in a row (§33)
- [ ] Mirrored rhythm check: no three paragraphs in a row that open the same way or have the same length
- [ ] House rules: verdict words exact; banned words 0 (safe, verified, authentic, genuine, certified, guaranteed, fake, counterfeit, certify); SANDBOX / TESTNET; every [FILL n] kept; no team names, school, age, visa, founder/CEO; Peel = "method only, no code; every line of our code was written this weekend"; nothing about internal tooling
