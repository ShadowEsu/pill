# Stats check: "1.3 million" and "98,000"

Checked 2026-10-24 (late Sat). Every URL below was opened, not taken from a search snippet.

## 1. "1.3 million people injured from correctly prescribed drugs"

**Verdict: use with this wording.** The owner's phrasing is wrong. The source counts injuries from medication ERRORS, not from drugs that were "correctly prescribed".

- **Exact quote (WHO, live page):** "Medication errors cause at least one death every day and injure approximately 1.3 million people annually in the United States of America alone."
  - URL: https://www.who.int/news/item/29-03-2017-who-launches-global-effort-to-halve-medication-related-errors-in-5-years
  - Date: 29 March 2017 (WHO news release, Global Patient Safety Challenge on Medication Safety)
- **Exact quote (FDA, archived page):** "Medication errors cause at least one death every day and injure approximately 1.3 million people annually in the United States."
  - The FDA page "Medication Error Reports" (CDER) is no longer live on fda.gov. Archived copy: https://web.archive.org/web/20161226210936/http://www.fda.gov/Drugs/DrugSafety/MedicationErrors/ucm080629.htm (posted 2009-03-05, last updated 10/20/2016)
  - The same page lists where errors happen: "prescribing, repackaging, dispensing, administering, or monitoring".
  - The current FDA page called "Working to Reduce Medication Errors" (https://www.fda.gov/drugs/information-consumers-and-patients-drugs/working-reduce-medication-errors) now returns 404. Its archived 2020 copy (https://web.archive.org/web/20201222072204/https://www.fda.gov/drugs/information-consumers-and-patients-drugs/working-reduce-medication-errors) does NOT contain the 1.3 million figure. It says FDA "receives more than 100,000 U.S. reports each year associated with a suspected medication error." Do not cite that page for 1.3 million.
- **What it means:** an estimate ("approximately") of US injuries each year from preventable medication errors anywhere in the chain: prescribing, dispensing, administering, monitoring, or patient use.
- **What it does NOT mean:**
  - It does not count harm from correctly prescribed drugs. Under the NCC MERP definition both FDA pages quote, a medication error is a "preventable event". It is a mistake, not a side effect of a correct prescription.
  - It says nothing about falsified or substandard medicines.
  - Neither page names the study behind the number, and both are years old (FDA page 2009–2016; WHO 2017).
- **Site-safe line:** "Medication errors injure approximately 1.3 million people a year in the United States, according to the World Health Organization (2017)."
- Cite the live WHO URL on the site. Keep the FDA archive link as backup.

## 2. "98,000 Americans"

**Verdict: use with this wording.** Always show the range, the year, and "medical errors in hospitals". Never write a bare "98,000".

- **Exact quote:** "When extrapolated to the over 33.6 million admissions to U.S. hospitals in 1997, the results of the study in Colorado and Utah imply that at least 44,000 Americans die each year as a result of medical errors. The results of the New York Study suggest the number may be as high as 98,000."
  - URL: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/9728/chapter/2 (Executive Summary, p. 1)
  - Report: Institute of Medicine, *To Err Is Human: Building a Safer Health System*. Released November 1999; the book is dated 2000. doi:10.17226/9728
- Same report, p. 2: "Medication errors alone, occurring either in or out of the hospital, are estimated to account for over 7,000 deaths annually." This is the drug-specific number from the same source.
- FDA's archived consumer page summarizes the report the same way: "between 44,000 and 98,000 deaths may result each year from medical errors in hospitals alone. And more than 7,000 deaths each year are related to medications." Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20180125215735/https://www.fda.gov/Drugs/ResourcesForYou/Consumers/ucm143553.htm
- **What it means:** a 1999 estimate of deaths per year in US hospitals from ALL medical errors (surgery, diagnosis, infection control, drugs, and more). It extrapolates two older studies to 1997 admissions. 98,000 is the upper bound and 44,000 is the lower bound.
- **What it does NOT mean:**
  - It is not about drugs alone. The drug-related figure is "over 7,000".
  - It says nothing about falsified medicines.
  - It is not a current number.
  - It does not cover people outside hospitals.
- **Site-safe line:** "A 1999 Institute of Medicine report estimated that medical errors in U.S. hospitals cause between 44,000 and 98,000 deaths a year."
- If the site needs a drug-specific figure, use this instead: "The same report estimated medication errors alone account for over 7,000 deaths a year."

## Framing note for Pill copy

Both numbers measure medication and medical ERRORS, not falsified medicines. Do not place either number next to a claim about bad or falsified pills in a way that suggests they measure that problem. Each number fits only as context: mistakes with medicines already hurt people at scale.
