## 90-second version (word for word, 220 words)

*Drop the chalk quarter tablet. Point at the blue line.*

This bottle says vitamin B2. Inside is chalk. Watch the blue line: the water clouds, the light drops, right into the reference band. A one-sensor tester would call that a pass.

*Point at the green line.*

But the green line never moves. Pill says NEEDS LAB, and the supplier's four hundred and eighty dollars stays held, with a lab referral already drafted.

*Look up at the judge.*

WHO estimates 1 in 10 medical products in low- and middle-income countries is substandard or falsified. A new lab dissolution tester costs twenty to a hundred thousand dollars, and lab results take seven to ten business days. A clinic pharmacist has neither. What they have is an invoice to pay this afternoon.

Pill is a forty-dollar box. One quarter tablet, about three minutes, two channels that must agree. Riboflavin absorbs blue and glows green. Chalk only blocks light.

It says three things only: PASS, NEEDS LAB, UNABLE TO VERIFY. Never a fourth.

And it decides the money. A policy agent holds the payment in escrow and proposes release only on a PASS; a pharmacist taps to approve. Claude explains the result and keeps the log, but never sets a verdict and never moves money. Other clinics can buy a verdict for five cents over x402. It's all sandbox and testnet money today.

Our ask: try to make it say PASS on the chalk.
